Assignment 18: Conceptual Design of an Adaptive Traffic Signal System

Case Problem & Engineering Objective

A four-arm urban arterial intersection in Bengaluru (Left-Hand Traffic, IRC SP:41 geometry) operates under fixed-time signals (30s equal splits), causing severe directional queue spillback during tidal peak surges and wasted green time during off-peak periods. Below is the complete 8-task conceptual design for an Adaptive Traffic Control System (ATCS) demonstrated in the live daylight 3D simulation.

1 Traffic Demand Variation Study (Diurnal & Directional Profile)

Classified volume counts converted into Passenger Car Units (PCU per IRC:106) reveal bimodal commuter peaks (08:30–10:30 AM and 17:30–20:00 PM) with strong tidal asymmetry: North–South arterial demand surges to 3,800 PCU/hr while East–West cross-street demand stays near 540 PCU/hr.

2 Suitable Traffic-Detection Technologies

Sensor Type Role & Accuracy Trade-Off
Inductive Loops (0–2m & 45m) 99% presence & occupancy accuracy; low latency (<20ms). Requires saw-cut pavement installation.
77 GHz FMCW Radar Multi-lane dilemma-zone speed & queue tracking up to 150m; all-weather. Higher per-arm hardware cost.
Overhead AI Edge Vision Multi-class PCU classification (Bus, Truck, Car, 2W) & spillback detection. Requires IR night/monsoon filtering.

3 Real-Time Traffic Parameters Measured

  • 1. Classified Flow Rate (q, PCU/hr): Weighted vehicle counts at 45m upstream detectors to compute directional demand ratios.
  • 2. Time Occupancy (O, %): Fraction of scan time a stop-line inductive loop is occupied; O > 35% signals queue saturation.
  • 3. Time Headway (h, sec): Inter-vehicle gap across the stop bar; h > 3.0s triggers early phase Gap-Out.
  • 4. Queue Length (L_q, meters): Real-time spatial queue extent between the 0m stop bar and 45m advance loop.
  • 5. Approach Speed (v, km/h): Spot speed used for dilemma-zone protection prior to amber onset.

4 Conceptual Adaptive Signal-Control Logic (Gap-Out & Surge Extension)

5 Proposed Sensor Location & Intersection Layout (LHT IRC SP:41)

Stop-Line Loops (0–2m): Placed immediately upstream of the white stop bar in each inbound lane to detect waiting queues and stop-bar headways.

Advance Detectors (45m): Placed 45m upstream to measure approaching platoons ~3.5s before arrival, enabling seamless green extensions without stopping main-street platoons.

Overhead Mast-Arm Gantry: Carries 1.6x high-visibility signal heads plus compact AI vision housings for multi-class PCU counting.

6 Three-Layer ITS Communication Architecture

Layer 1: Field Detection & Actuation

Inductive loop detector cards, 77GHz radar, overhead AI vision cameras, and LED signal heads wired via shielded RS-485/Ethernet to the roadside cabinet.

Layer 2: Edge ATCS Controller & 5G RSU

Industrial edge processor runs local vehicle-actuated gap-out/extension logic (<50ms latency) and streams telemetry via 5G / Fiber-Optic backhaul.

Layer 3: Central Traffic Control Centre (TCC)

Corridor green-wave offset coordination, incident overrides, BMTC transit signal priority (TSP), and live operator dashboard.

7 Signal Timing Modification Methodology

Every cycle, the ATCS recalculates critical flow ratios y_i = q_i / s_i (where saturation flow s_i ≈ 1,900 PCU/hr/lane) and updates Webster’s optimal cycle & green splits:

C_opt = (1.5 · L + 5) / (1 − Y),   Y = Σ y_i
g_i = clamp( (y_i / Y) · (C_opt − L),  g_min=10s,  g_max=55s )

During a North–South Surge, y_NS rises sharply relative to y_EW, shifting green split from 50%/50% (30s/30s) to 82%/18% (55s/12s) while early gap-out terminates E–W as soon as its 2-vehicle queue clears.

8 Expected Operational Benefits & Implementation Challenges

Operational Benefits
  • 55–68% Reduction in Control Delay: Cuts surge delay from >75s (LOS F) down to ~26s (LOS C).
  • Elimination of Upstream Spillback: Prevents N–S queues from blocking adjacent intersections.
  • 18–24% Lower Idling CO₂ & NOₓ Emissions: Fewer stop-start cycles for heavy BMTC buses and trucks.
  • Zero Wasted Off-Peak Green: Early gap-out compresses off-peak cycle times (~36s) for LOS B progression.
Implementation Challenges (Indian Urban Context)
  • Weak Lane Discipline & Two-Wheeler Filtering: Requires multi-zone virtual detection rather than single-lane point loops alone.
  • Utility Road-Cuts & Loop Maintenance: Frequent trenching can damage saw-cut inductive loops; paired overhead radar provides redundancy.
  • Power & Communication Uptime: Requires online UPS battery backup (4+ hrs) and dual-SIM 5G/fiber failover.
  • Mixed Traffic PCU Calibration: Dynamic PCU weights must be calibrated locally for buses, auto-rickshaws, and cars.
